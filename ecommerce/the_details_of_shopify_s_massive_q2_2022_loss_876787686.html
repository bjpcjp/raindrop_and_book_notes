The Details of Shopify’s Massive Q2 2022 Loss

Tags: ecommerce, shopify

Bookmark ID: 876787686

Shopify released on July 27 disappointing second-quarter financial results and provided an unfavorable forecast for the remainder of the year. Furthermore, on July 26 Shopify announced that it was laying off 10% of its employees — about 1,000 — due to slowing growth in ecommerce. Most layoffs will occur in the recruiting, support, and sales units.

In a message to employees, CEO Tobi Lütke said that Shopify “bet that the channel mix — the share of dollars that travel through ecommerce rather than physical retail — would permanently leap ahead by five or even 10 years. We couldn’t know for sure at the time, but we knew that if there were a chance that this was true, we would have to expand the company to match. It’s now clear that the bet didn’t pay off. I got this wrong.”

To save costs, Shopify announced that most of its employees would permanently work remotely so the company could repurpose or reconfigure its remaining office space and potentially terminate leases or sublet spaces.

Financial Results

While Shopify’s headquarters are in Canada, it reports financial results in U.S. dollars. The company reported a loss of three cents per share. Many analysts expected a three cents gain. The operating loss for the second quarter of 2022 was $190.2 million, or 15% of revenue, versus income of $139.4 million, or 12% of revenue, for the comparable period a year ago. Revenue for the second quarter was close to expectations, coming in at $1.3 billion versus an expected $1.33 billion. Shopify’s stock is down 74% in 2022.

While revenue increased almost 16% over the same quarter last year, operating costs ballooned more than 75%. Net loss for the second quarter was $1.2 billion versus a profit of $979 million for the second quarter of 2021. Sales and marketing expenses alone grew by 62.4%. Gross merchandise volume — the value of merchant sales flowing through Shopify’s platform — grew 11% to $46.9 billion during the quarter, missing estimates of $48.6 billion.

Shopify announced that merchant solutions revenue — earnings from credit-card processing, mostly —  rose 18.3% to $929 million. Merchant solutions are the largest source of the company’s operating cash. Subscription revenue — monthly fees for using the platform — grew 10% to $366.4 million. Analysts had projected merchant solutions revenue of $968.2 million and subscription solutions revenue of $361.6 million.

The cost of merchant solutions increased by $118.6 million, or 27.2%, for the three months ended June 30, 2022, compared to the same period in 2021. The increase was primarily due to higher interchange fees on payment processing.

The cost of subscription revenue increased by $22.2 million, or 35.2%, for the three months ended June 30, 2022, compared to the same period in 2021. The increase was due to the platform’s growth in traffic and functionality across global jurisdictions, resulting in additional cloud infrastructure and employee costs, according to Shopify.

In its earnings presentation, Shopify emphasized the positive aspects of its performance:

  • Monthly recurring revenue grew 13% year-over-year to $107.2 million in the second quarter as merchants joined the platform.
  • Shopify Plus attracted additional merchants, too, and the number of brick-and-mortar locations using the company’s point-of-sale feature — POS Pro — increased.
  • Offline gross merchandise volume grew 47% year-over-year.
  • GMV via partner integrations — Google, Facebook, Instagram — grew roughly 500% over the same quarter last year.
  • The acquisition of Deliverr, which closed on July 8, 2022, with 450 employees, will bolster the Shopify Fulfillment Network.
  • B2B functionality is now integrated into the Shopify platform, making it easier for brands on Shopify Plus to sell to other businesses via the same store they use for consumers.
  • In June, Shopify announced a partnership with the Twitter Shopping Channel, enabling merchants to reach consumers directly through their Twitter profiles, with buyers seeing products and pricing without leaving the application.
  • In the second quarter, the company launched Shopify Payments and Shopify Shipping in France, and point-of-sale functionality with integrated payments in Italy.
  • At the beginning of 2022, Shopify launched Markets, a cross-border management tool that allows merchants to establish, optimize, and manage international sales from a single store. The company says it has 100,000 merchants now offering a localized experience to their buyers.

Outlook

Shopify’s near-term outlook, according to the company’s spokespeople on the earnings call, includes:

  • Operating losses will likely continue in the last two quarters of 2022.
  • The growth of merchant solutions’ revenue year-over-year will presumably exceed the growth from subscription solutions.
  • The number of merchants joining the platform will be higher in the last two quarters of 2022 compared to the first two.
  • Revenue will grow faster than gross profit.
