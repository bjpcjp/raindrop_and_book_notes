How PawnGuru Helps Sellers And Pawn Shops Compare Prices Online

Pawning something you own can be a major challenge. You want to get the highest amount for your item, but you don’t know which pawn shop will pay the most. Reality TV shows like “Hardcore Pawn” and “Pawn Stars” demonstrate how pawn shops work, but a Michigan-based startup called PawnGuru has found a way to simplify that process by bringing the industry online.

According to co-founder Jordan Birnholtz, PawnGuru users post requests online to sell, pawn or buy items to and from local pawn shops. Birnholtz said their website lets sellers solicit offers and negotiate prices online with multiple pawn shops so they can get the best deal possible.

PawnGuru started in the summer of 2014, when its founders visited 10 pawn shops in Detroit with several items. They wanted to see if each pawn shop would offer the same amount of money as the others. Instead, they found tremendous inequality. One of the items -- a diamond ring -- received offers as low as $65 and as high $1,060. “When we saw that much variance, we knew there was an opportunity,” said Birnholtz.

PawnGuru is free for consumers to use. According to Birnholtz, pawn shops like using PawnGuru because it helps them win business. James Crawford, the owner of Heritage Jewelry and Loan in Houston, Texas, said that PawnGuru is "the only online marketing service for pawn shops that actually works.”

In a study of its own pawn shop data, PawnGuru confirmed that big price differences are typical of the industry, even within a single city. Birnholtz said that they found an average 260% price spread for a given item across all categories. For some categories, like jewelry, that spread was even higher – 500% on average. Birnholtz claims that people who want or need to use pawn shops don’t have time to shop around for prices, which is a process that PawnGuru simplifies. Birnholtz said that the discrepancies between pawn shops exist for a few reasons: “Because different pawn shops are more knowledgeable about certain kinds of items, because more efficient pawn shops can offer more money and because pawn shops have different amounts of capital."

While bringing pawn shops online, Birnholtz said they discovered an opportunity bigger than just pawn loans -- which is a $15 billion per year industry. “Pawn shops have cash on hand, inventory and storage space. That makes them a great basis for an online, local market.” Today, nearly three quarters of PawnGuru’s customers are looking to buy or sell an item – using the service as a safe and convenient alternative to Craigslist or eBay .

How does PawnGuru work? Users fill out a form online for each item that they want to sell and upload photos. Then the item is offered to local pawnshops first to see if they want to make an offer. Questions can be answered online and prices can be negotiated. After the price is agreed upon, the seller can take the item for sale to the pawn shop to close the deal. As of right now, PawnGuru is available for free. However, PawnGuru does not process the payments.

PawnGuru has signed up more than 1,300 pawn shops over the past year and a half, serving 23,000 consumers across the U.S. that have negotiated around $4.2 million in items. PawnGuru has grown about 30% every month since the site launched this past summer. The plan is eventually for PawnGuru to take a cut of the transactions between the pawn shops and the sellers.

Founded by David Stiebel and Jonathan Polter, PawnGuru raised $800,000 in funding from angel investors thus far. The First Step Fund (Invest Detroit) and Impact America provided the seed round of funding. PawnGuru is currently looking into raising a $2 million round so that it can expand into more cities. PawnGuru will also use the funding to open its site to thrift stores and antique shops.

Stiebel attended MIT and worked in the consulting industry for a while before joining up with Polter in Detroit, where they co-founded PawnGuru. Birnholtz joined shortly after, introduced by a PawnGuru investor who heard his pitch for another startup.
