Shaping the future of power electronics: GaN’s expansion across key high-growth markets

The next wave of expansion is expected in the automotive market, where the shift towards electrification and advanced driver assistance systems is pushing the limits of conventional power devices. GaN devices are widely utilized today in LiDAR systems, and GaN onboard chargers (OBCs) are anticipated to be the next volume driver. Off-board DC chargers and traction inverters are reaching maturity as more reference designs continue to emerge. The automotive and mobility market is projected to grow at an outstanding CAGR of 73% from 2024 to 2030.

Similarly, hyperscale data centers are seeking energy-efficient solutions to manage ever-growing workloads. NVIDIA is partnering with leading wide-bandgap chipmakers to integrate SiC and GaN technologies into its 800 V HVDC power systems, paving the way for large-scale GaN adoption in data centers within the next three to four years. GaN is uniquely positioned to support these architectures by enabling more efficient power conversion and freeing up valuable board space. For AI servers and networking equipment, the adoption of GaN is becoming a competitive necessity. Together with telecom, the market is set to grow at a remarkable 53% CAGR between 2024 and 2030.

The GaN power race: from market leaders to new entrants

Since 2023, the power GaN ecosystem has entered a decisive phase of consolidation and expansion, starting with Infineon’s $830 million purchase of GaN Systems and Renesas’ $339 million acquisition of Transphorm.

With consolidation setting the stage, attention now shifts to how companies are positioning themselves to define the next phase of the GaN ecosystem.

Roy Dagher Yole Group

Yole Group confirms the transition of the power GaN ecosystem from maturity to a strategic, IDM-driven market shaped by consolidation and vertical integration.

  • Innoscience remains the leader with 30% market share in 2024, fueled by design wins in fast chargers, automotive, data center, and home appliances. Beyond devices, the company also develops epi wafers, strengthening vertical integration. It remains highly active in China while extending its reach abroad through its partnership with STMicroelectronics.
  • Renesas, through Transphorm, now offers a broad portfolio spanning low-voltage e-mode (40–200 V) to high-voltage d-mode (650 V) devices and is on track to surpass $100M in GaN revenues by 2026.
  • Infineon, a long-time power electronics leader, has strengthened its GaN position via CoolGaN™ products and GaN Systems acquisition. With wins across consumer, data center, and automotive markets, Infineon is collaborating with NVIDIA and developing 12-inch GaN-on-Si pilot lines.
  • Navitas, a fabless pioneer, is diversifying beyond consumer into high-power markets with GaNSafe. It secured deals with Enphase and NVIDIA and achieved the first OBC adoption of GaN in Qiyuan’s SUV E07.
  • Power Integrations, leveraging GaN-on-Sapphire PowiGaN®, has built a strong portfolio, expanded into higher voltages (1250 V and 1700 V), and is growing revenues rapidly with design wins across multiple sectors.
  • EPC, the GaN pioneer, continues to innovate with an extensive e-mode portfolio and new designs for robotics and space, reinforcing its role as a key low-voltage GaN supplier.

Foundries also capture market share and will continue to play an essential role in the ecosystem. Despite TSMC’s announced exit, other foundries such as Polar Semi and PSMC have joined the race, while Global Foundries, X-FAB, and Vanguard are expanding their GaN portfolios. Meanwhile, Samsung is preparing a GaN release for 2026. As for onsemi, the company has remained silent; its 2024 GaN technical paper and strong positioning in Si and SiC make its entry into GaN inevitable. Based on our internal assessment, onsemi is preparing to join the GaN market soon.

To conclude this snapshot, Yole Group confirms the transition of the power GaN ecosystem from maturity to a strategic, IDM-driven market shaped by consolidation and vertical integration. With newcomers like Samsung and onsemi set to enter, competition will intensify, cementing GaN as a core pillar of next-generation power electronics.
