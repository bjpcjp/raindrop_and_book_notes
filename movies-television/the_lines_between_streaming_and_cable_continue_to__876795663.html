Same ol’, same ol’

Streaming platforms acting more like traditional cable seems heavily tied to revenue and ad-related interests. However, this is also reflective of customer demand, which is being impacted by subscription fatigue and some belief that the golden era of premium streaming content has passed its peak.

The popularity of YouTube TV, which is essentially cable but delivered through the Internet, also highlights interest in the cross-section between Internet-based and traditional TV. YouTube TV is the US's fourth-biggest cable TV provider. YouTube, including YouTube TV, is also the biggest streaming service in the country based on time spent, per February Nielsen data.

Although people have lamented the days of having "57 channels and nothin' on," viewers are increasingly turning to alternatives, like FAST TV, to deal with dissatisfaction with on-demand streaming services. With streaming platforms' rotating and sometimes overstuffed libraries, some are even nostalgic for channel surfing or prefer it. FAST TV service Tubi garners more viewing time than Max and Peacock and could eventually surpass Disney+, Bloomberg recently calculated using Nielsen data.

“Reach without viewership does not add up to enough ad opportunities to help clients achieve [desired] outcomes,” Brian Albert, Google's managing director of US video who's responsible for YouTube upfront ad deals, told The Information.

As the lines between cable and streaming continue to blur, one can hope that the industry settles on something that combines the best of both worlds in order to cater to subscriber needs and deliver unprecedented value. Unfortunately, streaming platforms' current top priorities—based on statements by executives, earning calls, and the like—seem to be around growing the average revenue earned per user. And traditional TV has already written a playbook for that.

Years ago, it seemed like streaming would pave a new path forward. Technology made the possibilities of on-demand content exciting; streaming platforms kept delivering impressive libraries with can't-miss exclusives, and prices were stable, low, and simple. Cable's continuously declining customer base is a reminder that viewers want something different.

But today, streaming platforms appear to be diverging from that future in favor of old-school methods. Without more innovation, what will be left for subscribers is more of the same ol', same ol'.
