A/B testing is a very popular technique for checking granular changes in a product without mistakenly taking into account changes that were caused by outside factors. In this series of articles, I will try to give an easy hands-on manual on how to design, run and estimate results of a/b tests, so you are ready to go and get these amazing statistically significant results!

In the previous articles, we’ve discussed the frequentist approach to A/B testing:

Parts one and two give an overview of experiment design and the most common probability distributions. In parts three and four we talked about two different types of tests (parametric and non-parametric) and when and how to use them.

Frequentist vs Bayesian approaches

As good as it is, the traditional frequentist statistical approach has its shortcomings. The first (and usually the most annoying for practitioners) is that you need a lot of data to get statistically significant results. And the smaller the changes you want to detect, the more data you need! For instance, in the first post of this series, we have estimated that it will require at least 2305 users in test and control groups to detect a 2% difference in day-1 retention.
