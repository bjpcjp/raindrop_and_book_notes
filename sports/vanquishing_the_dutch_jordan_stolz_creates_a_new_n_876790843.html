Stolz, the 18-year-old from Wisconsin, won three gold medals at the speedskating world championships, finishing his turns in a way that seemed like something out of a storybook.

Published March 5, 2023Updated April 17, 2023

HEERENVEEN, the Netherlands — On the breathtaking final curve of his 500-meter race at speedskating’s world championships Friday night, Jordan Stolz well and truly arrived as the future of speedskating.

The 18-year-old from Kewaskum, Wis., whipped around the south end of Thialf, the national oval in the Netherlands, to become the youngest skater ever to win a title at the single-distance championships. By the time the weekend was over, Stolz had also won gold medals in the 1,000-meter and 1,500-meter contests, becoming the first man to ever win three individual races at single-distance championships.

The loudest Thialf got all weekend was in the final pairing of the 1,500 meters on Sunday, when Dutch world-record holder Kjeld Nuis was skating slightly ahead of the time Stolz had set earlier in the day. Over 12,000 fans were on their feet, screaming for Nuis to beat the kid who refused to lose.

He could not.

But as much as Thialf buzzed over the four-day event about Stolz’s future, and now his present, it was how he skated that curve on Friday that left the speedskating world flabbergasted and desperate for more.

Numerous competitors — even those who did not skate against Stolz and even when they were not prompted — voiced their astonishment at his performance. The coach of one of the Netherlands’s leading professional teams was said to have watched video of Stolz’s curve over and over.
